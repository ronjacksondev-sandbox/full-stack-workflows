Duo Budget – Idea Validation Plan

Purpose

Validate whether the core idea—connecting time and money through shared categories—resonates with real people enough to justify moving into product discovery. This phase answers the question: “Should we build anything at all?”

Hypotheses

  • People struggle to understand where their time goes.
  • People struggle to understand where their money goes.
  • People see time and money as related resources.
  • People would benefit from viewing time and money together.
  • People would be willing to try a tool that unifies them.
  • Some people would pay for this clarity.

Signals of validation

  • Clear, repeated pain points around time and money tracking.
  • Evidence that people already compare time versus money in decisions.
  • Interest in a unified model (not confusion or resistance).
  • Positive reactions to the concept statement.
  • At least some willingness to pay.
  • Early adopters expressing desire to try a prototype.

Methods

Concept testing

Show a short concept statement and measure reactions.

Survey

Gather broad patterns and validate assumptions.

Interviews:

Explore mental models and deeper reasoning.

Problem-space validation

Ask about problems without mentioning solutions.

Lightweight landing page

Test interest with a simple early-access CTA.

Participants

Target participants

  • People who track money.
  • People who track time.
  • People who plan, budget, or self-organize.
  • People who DIY projects to save money.
  • People who think about time as a resource.

Excluded participants

  • People who do not track time or money.
  • People uninterested in planning or self-management.

Key questions to answer

  • Do people feel pain around time tracking.
  • Do people feel pain around money tracking.
  • Do they see these as connected.
  • Do they already make time-versus-money tradeoffs.
  • Does the concept of shared categories make sense.
  • Does the unified model feel valuable or confusing.
  • Would they try a tool like this.
  • Would they pay for it.

Success criteria

  • 60%+ of participants express frustration with time or money tracking.
  • 40%+ say they compare time versus money in decisions.
  • 50%+ say a unified model sounds useful.
  • 20%+ say they would pay for it.
  • 10 or more people sign up for early access from the landing page.
  • Interview participants express curiosity or excitement.

Risks and invalidation signals

  • People do not see time and money as related.
  • People find the unified model confusing.
  • People prefer existing tools and do not want to switch.
  • People do not feel pain around tracking.
  • People do not want another tool in their life.
  • No one expresses willingness to try or pay.

Timeline

  • Week 1: Draft concept statement, survey, and landing page.
  • Week 2: Collect survey responses and run interviews.
  • Week 3: Synthesize findings and decide whether to proceed.

Deliverables

  • Concept statement.
  • Survey results.
  • Interview notes.
  • Landing page metrics.
  • Research synthesis document.
  • Go/No-Go recommendation.
